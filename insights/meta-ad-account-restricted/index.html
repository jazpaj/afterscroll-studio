What To Do If Your Meta Ad Account Is Restricted

A calm, ordered response: read the notice properly, fix the underlying cause, then appeal once with a complete case.

By Afterscroll Platform Desk17 April 20268 min readPlatform Ops

Restrictions are usually automated and often reversible, but panic makes them worse. Creating new accounts, appealing repeatedly or moving assets between business portfolios tends to compound the problem. Work the process instead.

Step 1 — Read the actual notice

Find the restriction in Account Quality and note exactly what is restricted: the ad account, the business portfolio, the Page, a specific ad, or the personal profile of an admin. The remedy differs entirely. Note the policy cited, because your appeal must address that policy specifically.

Step 2 — Fix the cause before appealing

  • Review recent ads against the cited policy — particularly personal attributes, health claims, before/after imagery, financial promises and prohibited products.
  • Check landing pages: policy applies to the destination, including anything the page links to.
  • Verify billing: a failed payment or unusual payment change triggers restrictions that look like policy issues.
  • Check business and domain verification status.
  • Confirm admin accounts have two-factor enabled and no unusual login activity.

Step 3 — Appeal once, properly

  1. State what you believe was flagged and why it complies, referencing the specific policy language.
  2. Describe what you changed, if anything, and where it can be verified.
  3. Include business documentation and domain ownership evidence for the business portfolio.
  4. Keep it factual and short. Submit once and wait for a decision before submitting again.

While you wait

Shift budget to other channels, keep organic running, and don't attempt to run the same creative through a different account — that pattern is detected and usually widens the restriction. Use the time to build tracking and creative infrastructure.

Prevention that works

A clean business portfolio, verified business and domain, two-factor on all admins, redundant admin access, and a pre-flight policy check on creative before launch.
