Why Your Facebook Ads Stopped Converting

Performance rarely collapses for one reason. Work through the diagnostic in this order and you'll find the actual cause faster.

By Afterscroll Media Desk11 September 20268 min readPaid Media

When an account that worked stops working, the instinct is to blame the algorithm or the ads. Usually the cause is further upstream. Run the checks in dependency order — measurement, then audience saturation, then creative, then offer, then experience — because fixing a later layer while an earlier one is broken just produces confident nonsense.

1. Is your measurement still telling the truth?

  • Check event volume against actual orders in your backend for the last 30 days. A 20%+ gap needs explaining.
  • Look for duplicate events, a broken server-side integration, or a consent banner change that quietly reduced matched conversions.
  • Confirm nothing changed in your domain verification, aggregated event configuration or checkout flow.

A measurement break looks exactly like a performance drop, and it sends you optimising against a distorted picture.

2. Have you saturated the audience you actually have?

Rising frequency with flat reach means you're paying more to talk to the same people. Check frequency at the campaign level over a 14-day window and compare new-customer share of revenue against previous months. If new-customer share is falling while spend rises, the account is grinding rather than growing.

3. Has the creative gone stale, or was it always narrow?

Ad fatigue is real but overdiagnosed. The deeper problem is usually that every asset makes the same argument. If all your winners share one hook and one angle, you don't have a creative library — you have one ad in six outfits. Build variants that differ in argument, not just in edit.

4. Has the market's offer moved?

Competitors change pricing, bundling, shipping thresholds and guarantees constantly. Your ad can be perfect and still lose to a better deal presented plainly. Audit three competitors' landing pages and checkout terms before you blame your hook rate.

5. Does the page still match the ad?

  • Load your top three landing pages on a mid-range phone on mobile data and time them honestly.
  • Check that the claim, image and price in the ad appear above the fold on the page.
  • Look at scroll depth and rage-click data if you have it — a page that changed last quarter may have quietly broken the match.

A useful habit

Keep a dated change log for the account: creative launches, budget shifts, site releases, price changes, tracking edits. When performance moves, the answer is usually in that log.

Work the order. The accounts that recover fastest are the ones where someone resisted the urge to change five things on day one.
