How Brands Can Use Social Listening Properly

Listening is only valuable if it changes something. Wire it into creative briefs, product decisions and support — not a monthly slide.

By Afterscroll Strategy Desk29 May 20267 min readStrategy

Most social listening produces a sentiment chart nobody acts on. The value isn't in measuring mood; it's in harvesting the exact language customers use about problems you can solve, then putting that language somewhere it does work.

What to listen for

  • Objections stated in the wild — the reasons people give for not buying.
  • Comparison language — how people describe you against alternatives.
  • Use cases you didn't design for, which are often your best new positioning.
  • Recurring confusion, which is a landing page or FAQ problem.
  • Emerging formats and references your audience is already fluent in.

Where the output should land

  1. Creative briefs: verbatim quotes become hooks.
  2. Landing pages: the top three objections get answered above the fold.
  3. Product and merchandising: recurring requests get a decision, not a note.
  4. Support macros: the same confusion answered once, well.
  5. Retention messaging: the words people use when something goes right.

A light operating rhythm

Twenty minutes a week reading comments on your own and competitors' content, plus a monthly pull from reviews and support tickets, gets you most of the value without a platform contract. Keep a running document of verbatim quotes tagged by theme; it becomes the most-used file in your marketing team.

The test of a listening program

If nothing in your creative or your site changed because of it this quarter, it isn't a listening program — it's a subscription.
